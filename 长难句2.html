Master Complex Sentences for Higher Band Scores

This resource provides 20 complex sentence structures frequently seen in high-scoring IELTS responses. Each example is followed by two practice sentences with similar grammatical structures.

To improve your writing: Study the examples, analyze the structure, then practice creating your own sentences using the patterns shown.

1
Conditional + Concession
Although renewable energy sources are becoming increasingly affordable, many countries continue to rely on fossil fuels, which suggests that economic factors alone do not determine energy policies.
Although electric vehicles offer significant environmental benefits, many consumers still prefer traditional cars, which indicates that other factors like charging infrastructure influence purchasing decisions.
While remote work has gained popularity during the pandemic, many companies insist on office-based work, which demonstrates that organizational culture plays a crucial role in work arrangements.
Structure: Although + [subject + verb], [main clause] + which + [verb] + that + [clause]
2
Causal Relationship
The rapid urbanization witnessed in developing nations has resulted in unprecedented pressure on infrastructure, thereby creating challenges that require innovative solutions from urban planners.
The widespread adoption of social media has transformed communication patterns, thereby altering how people form and maintain relationships.
Increased international tourism has boosted local economies in many regions, thereby creating employment opportunities that benefit entire communities.
Structure: [Subject] + has resulted in + [effect], thereby + [gerund] + [complement]
3
Comparative + Result
The more governments invest in preventative healthcare measures, the less burden there will be on acute care services, a situation that would benefit both patients and healthcare systems.
The more time students dedicate to practical applications of theory, the better their understanding becomes, an outcome that enhances long-term knowledge retention.
The earlier children are exposed to multiple languages, the more naturally they acquire them, an advantage that can persist throughout their lives.
Structure: The more + [subject + verb], the less/more + [noun] + [clause], a situation/outcome that + [clause]
4
Passive Voice + Relative Clause
Cultural heritage sites are often preserved through international cooperation, a practice which not only safeguards history but also promotes mutual understanding between nations.
Endangered species are frequently protected by cross-border agreements, an approach that requires coordination between multiple governments and conservation groups.
Technological innovations are increasingly developed through global collaboration, a process that accelerates progress by combining expertise from different regions.
Structure: [Subject] + are often + [past participle] + [prepositional phrase], a practice/approach which + [clause]
5
Not only... but also...
Not only does globalization facilitate the exchange of goods and services, but it also enables the dissemination of ideas across cultural boundaries, thereby enriching societies worldwide.
Not only does regular exercise improve physical health, but it also enhances mental well-being by reducing stress and anxiety levels.
Not only has digital technology transformed how we work, but it has also revolutionized how we socialize and form relationships.
Structure: Not only + [inverted auxiliary + subject] + [verb], but it also + [verb] + [complement]
6
Despite + Gerund
Despite having access to unprecedented technological resources, many students struggle with basic research skills, a paradox that educators are working to resolve.
Despite receiving extensive training, many employees find it challenging to adapt to new software systems, a difficulty that often requires additional support.
Despite living in an information-rich society, many people lack critical media literacy skills, a deficiency that makes them vulnerable to misinformation.
Structure: Despite + [gerund phrase], [main clause], a paradox/difficulty that + [clause]
7
Such... that...
Climate change presents such an existential threat to coastal communities that comprehensive adaptation strategies must be developed with urgency.
Artificial intelligence has developed such sophisticated capabilities that ethical guidelines for its use have become increasingly necessary.
The pandemic caused such significant disruptions to education systems that innovative approaches to learning had to be rapidly implemented.
Structure: Such + [adjective + noun] + that + [consequence clause]
8
It is... that... (Emphasis)
It is precisely this adaptability to changing circumstances that distinguishes successful organizations from those that fail to evolve.
It is the ability to learn from failure that often determines long-term success in entrepreneurial ventures.
It is the preservation of indigenous languages that maintains cultural diversity in an increasingly globalized world.
Structure: It is + [emphasized element] + that + [verb] + [complement]
9
Rather than + Gerund
Rather than focusing exclusively on economic indicators, policymakers should consider holistic measures of national progress, including but not limited to environmental sustainability and social well-being.
Rather than relying solely on examinations, educators should employ diverse assessment methods that capture different learning styles.
Rather than banning new technologies outright, regulators should develop frameworks that maximize benefits while minimizing risks.
Structure: Rather than + [gerund], [subject] + should + [verb] + [object], including but not limited to + [examples]
10
So as to + Infinitive
Urban planning must incorporate green spaces so as to mitigate the urban heat island effect, a phenomenon whereby cities experience higher temperatures than surrounding rural areas.
Companies should invest in employee training programs so as to enhance workforce skills, an approach that ultimately boosts productivity and innovation.
Governments need to implement early warning systems so as to reduce disaster-related casualties, a strategy that has proven effective in numerous countries.
Structure: [Subject] + must + [verb] + [object] + so as to + [purpose], a phenomenon/approach whereby + [explanation]
11
Provided that + Condition
Technological advancement will continue to benefit humanity provided that ethical considerations are prioritized, without which innovation may cause unintended harm.
Economic globalization can be advantageous provided that fair trade practices are established, without which wealth disparities may worsen.
Artificial intelligence systems will gain public trust provided that transparency in decision-making is ensured, without which adoption may be limited.
Structure: [Subject] + will + [verb] + provided that + [condition], without which + [negative consequence]
12
What + Clause + Is...
What distinguishes effective leaders from merely competent managers is the ability to inspire others toward a shared vision, a quality that cannot be reduced to technical skills alone.
What makes a city truly livable is not just its infrastructure but the sense of community among its residents, an intangible yet crucial factor.
What determines the success of educational reforms is often teacher engagement rather than policy design, a reality that policymakers sometimes overlook.
Structure: What + [verb] + [subject] + is + [complement], a quality/factor that + [clause]
13
In order for... to...
In order for sustainable development goals to be achieved, it is imperative that governments, businesses, and civil society collaborate in unprecedented ways.
In order for renewable energy to replace fossil fuels, it is essential that substantial investments be made in storage technology and grid infrastructure.
In order for telemedicine to reach its full potential, it is crucial that digital literacy and internet access be improved in underserved communities.
Structure: In order for + [noun] + to + [verb], it is imperative that + [subject] + [verb] + [complement]
14
Not... until...
The true implications of automation on employment patterns may not be fully understood until several decades have passed, by which time workforce transitions may prove challenging to manage.
The benefits of early childhood education may not become apparent until children reach adolescence, at which point the foundation has already been established.
The ecological impact of certain chemicals may not be evident until they accumulate in the food chain, a process that can take years to manifest.
Structure: [Subject] + may not + [verb] + until + [time clause], by which time + [consequence]
15
As... as... Comparison
Urbanization is as much an opportunity for sustainable development as it is a challenge, requiring careful planning to maximize benefits while minimizing negative impacts.
Artificial intelligence is as much a tool for enhancing human capabilities as it is a potential disruptor, necessitating thoughtful integration into society.
Globalization is as much a cultural phenomenon as it is an economic process, influencing values and lifestyles worldwide.
Structure: [Subject] + is as much + [noun1] + as it is + [noun2], [gerund] + to + [purpose]
16
Whether... or... Contrast
Whether through government regulation or industry self-policing, data privacy standards must be strengthened to protect consumers in the digital age.
Whether by implementing carbon taxes or subsidizing green technology, nations must take decisive action to address climate change.
Whether through formal education or workplace training, continuous skill development has become essential in today's rapidly changing job market.
Structure: Whether + [prepositional phrase 1] or [prepositional phrase 2], [subject] + must + [verb] + [complement]
17
So... that... Result
The pace of technological change has become so rapid that traditional educational models struggle to keep pace, necessitating more adaptive approaches to curriculum development.
Microplastic pollution has become so pervasive that it has been detected in the most remote ecosystems, raising concerns about long-term environmental health.
Urban housing costs have risen so dramatically that key workers are increasingly priced out of city centers, creating workforce challenges for essential services.
Structure: [Subject] + has become so + [adjective] + that + [result clause], [gerund] + [complement]
18
The fact that... Demonstrates
The fact that biodiversity continues to decline at alarming rates demonstrates the inadequacy of current conservation efforts, highlighting the need for more radical approaches.
The fact that income inequality persists despite economic growth demonstrates that wealth does not automatically trickle down, challenging conventional economic theories.
The fact that mental health issues are increasing in affluent societies demonstrates that material prosperity alone does not ensure well-being, pointing to more complex determinants of health.
Structure: The fact that + [subject] + [verb] + demonstrates + [conclusion], [gerund] + [complement]
19
Inasmuch as + Qualification
Economic sanctions can be effective policy tools inasmuch as they pressure regimes without resorting to military force; nevertheless, their humanitarian consequences must be carefully weighed.
Standardized testing provides useful benchmarks inasmuch as it offers comparable data across institutions; however, it often fails to capture important aspects of learning.
Automation increases productivity inasmuch as it performs routine tasks efficiently; nonetheless, it creates challenges for workforce adaptation that require policy responses.
Structure: [Subject] + can be + [adjective] + inasmuch as + [qualification]; nevertheless, + [counterpoint]
20
Were... to... Conditional
Were governments to invest substantially in renewable energy infrastructure, not only would carbon emissions decrease, but new employment opportunities would emerge in green technologies.
Were educational institutions to prioritize critical thinking over rote memorization, students would develop more adaptable skills for an uncertain future.
Were companies to adopt more flexible work arrangements, both employee satisfaction and productivity would likely increase, benefiting all stakeholders.
Structure: Were + [subject] + to + [verb], not only would + [result1], but + [result2] + would + [verb]
